Privacy policy

Last updated 19 October 2026 · Version française

This policy explains what Wordsmith collects, why, who helps us run the game, how long things are kept and what you can do about it. It covers the Android app and the browser version at play.wordsmith-forge.workers.dev.

The short version

  • We collect what the game needs to work: your sign-in, your username and emblem, your progress, your friends and your Co-op and Versus results.
  • Text chat isn't saved. It stays in the room only while the room is open. Voice and video go live between players and are never recorded.
  • No ads, no analytics or tracking tools, and we never sell your data.
  • You can delete your account and its data at any time, in the app or at /delete-account.
  • Online play is for 13 and over (14 and over in Quebec). Players under 18, and everyone in Quebec, join rooms with their microphone muted.

Who we are

Wordsmith is a free, independent word game with a blacksmithing theme. In this policy, “we”, “us” and “the developer” mean the developer of Wordsmith. You can reach us with the contact form. Wordsmith is offered in the United States and Canada, and isn't offered in the European Union or the United Kingdom.

Person in charge of protecting personal information: the developer of Wordsmith, as privacy officer. Reach them through the contact form, choosing Privacy and my data.

What we collect and why

Your sign-in

You sign in with Google, or with an email address and password, through Firebase Authentication, a Google service. Firebase keeps your email address and looks after your password: it goes straight from the game to Firebase, and we never see it. With Google sign-in, Google also shares your account's basic profile (such as your name and picture) with Firebase; Wordsmith doesn't use them.

Whenever the game asks our server about your account, it sends a sign-in token from Firebase that proves who you are. Our server checks it and knows you by a random account ID. Our game database doesn't store your email address.

Why: so you can sign in on any phone or browser and keep your progress.

Your username and emblem

The username you choose and the emblem you pick from the game's set. Usernames must be unique and are checked for offensive words. We also note when you last changed your username.

Why: so other players know who they're playing with.

Your age

When you set up an account, we ask the month and year you were born (never the day). Accounts made before we asked are asked once.

Why: online play is for 13 and over, and 14 and over in Quebec, where the law needs a parent's consent for anyone younger. Players under 18 get safer settings: their microphone starts muted in every room. Your age is never shown to other players. If you're under the minimum age, we don't make the account: we delete what sign-in had created, and online play stays closed on that phone.

Your progress

Your level and XP (with a record of each XP award), your hints, your Scrap Bin, your stats (such as words forged and your longest word), your campaign and Master Forge progress, the Daily Commission days you've completed, your Daily Stoke, Work Orders and Weekly Trials, your Masterwork blueprint pieces, and your Crucible runs (puzzles cleared, words found and saves, and your best run).

When you finish a puzzle, the game sends the words you found, the hints you used and how long it took, so the server can check the result and award XP. If you played before signing in, those results carry a random ID the game made on that phone, which we remember (up to 5 per account).

Why: your progress lives on our server so it's safe if you change phones, and so XP and hints can't be faked.

Friends

Your friends and friend requests, the gifts you send and receive, and any nicknames you give your friends. Only you see the nicknames you set: the game shows them to you instead of your friends' usernames, including in notifications you get.

Why: to run the friends list, invites, gifts and the friends leaderboard.

Co-op and Versus

  • Match results: for each Co-op round and Versus series, who played, the points, words found, longest word, placing, rounds won and XP for each player, and the puzzle and difficulty.
  • Puzzles you've seen: which multiplayer and Daily Commission puzzles you've had, and when, so rooms can avoid giving you one you've had in the last 60 days.
  • While a room is open, the server holds what the game needs: the players' usernames, emblems and levels, the scores and the latest chat. This is wiped when the room closes (30 minutes after its last activity, or 2 minutes after everyone has left).

Text chat

Lines you write in a room go to everyone in that room. Our server tidies them and stars out swear words. The room keeps only its latest 50 lines, so players who join or reconnect can catch up, and they're gone when the room closes. Chat is never written to our database.

The one exception: when a player reports someone, up to 20 of that person's chat lines, as the reporter saw them, are sent with the report.

Voice and video

Your voice, and your camera if you turn it on, go live to the other players in the room through LiveKit Cloud. They never pass through our own servers, and they're never recorded. LiveKit is given your account ID and username so others can see who's talking. Voice chat is on unless you turn it off in Settings, and it starts when you join a room. Players under 18, and everyone playing from Quebec, always join with their microphone muted and turn it on themselves. Other adults start with the microphone as they last left it (on, the first time). Your camera stays off until you turn it on in the room. In the Android app, voice keeps working if you switch to another app while you're in a room, and a notification shows it's on; leaving the room or closing the game stops it.

Notifications (Android)

If you allow notifications, Firebase Cloud Messaging gives your phone a token, which we keep (for up to 5 phones per account) to send room invites, gifts from friends and the daily reminder. If you turn the reminder on, we also keep the hour you chose. Notifications include the friend's name and, for invites, the room code. The token is removed when you sign out or turn notifications off. The browser version doesn't use notifications.

Blocks and reports

The players you've blocked, and when. If you report someone, we keep who made the report, who it's about, their username at the time, the reason, any details you add (up to 500 characters), the room code and up to 20 of their chat lines. The player you block or report isn't told.

Why: to keep chat friendly and act on players who break the rules.

Some decisions are automatic: our filters star out swear words in chat and refuse offensive usernames. Everything else, such as acting on a report or removing an account, is decided by a person. You can ask us to look again at any decision about you through the contact form.

Messages you send us

If you write to us with the contact form, we keep the topic you choose and what you write, plus your email address and Wordsmith username if you give them. To stop the form being flooded, we also keep a scrambled form (a hash) of your IP address for a day, which only lets us count how many messages came from it. The message is emailed to the developer through Resend, an email delivery service. If you left an email address, we reply to it by email.

Why: to answer you and sort out what you asked about.

Crash reports

Nothing is sent automatically. On Android, if the game closed unexpectedly last time, it tells you and offers to copy a crash report: the game's version, your phone's make and model and Android version, when and how the game closed, the graphics mode, memory use, the last few errors the game noted and a technical trace. It's plain text that stays on your phone unless you paste it into a message yourself.

Word meanings

Most meanings come from a dictionary built into the game. For rarer words, the game asks our server, which looks the word up at the Free Dictionary API (dictionaryapi.dev) and then Wiktionary. Only the word is sent: the request doesn't carry your account, and the dictionaries hear from our server, not from your phone. Hearing a word said aloud uses the text-to-speech built into your phone or browser.

Technical data

Like any online service, our servers at Cloudflare receive your IP address and basic details of each request, such as the address asked for, the time, and your app or browser. Cloudflare uses these to deliver and protect the service. Our server's logs, which can include these details and error messages, are kept for a few days (at most 7).

Our server also works out from your IP address whether you're connecting from Quebec, so it can apply Quebec's rules (online play from 14, microphone muted by default). It's worked out each time and never stored. We don't use your location for anything else, and the game has no other location features.

What stays only on your phone

These are kept on your phone (or, in the browser version, in your browser's storage for the game's site) and never sent to us:

  • Your settings: sound, music and ambience volumes, vibration, colour scheme and font, left-handed layout, reduce motion, and the voice chat, text chat and notification switches.
  • Your puzzle history: the latest 150 puzzles you've finished on that phone, with their boards (in Co-op, who forged which word).
  • Which tips you've seen, the players you've muted in a room (for that room only) and the crash information described above.

The phone also keeps a copy of your profile, the puzzle you're in the middle of and results waiting to be sent, so you can play offline; these go to our server as described above. Firebase keeps you signed in on that phone or browser.

What other players see

  • Anyone signed in who knows your username can look up your public profile: username, emblem, level and rank, campaign progress and stats.
  • Your friends also see whether you're online, playing a puzzle or in a room, the rooms you have open, and where you are on the friends leaderboard and in the Weekly Trials.
  • Players in a room with you see your username, emblem and level, your scores, your chat, emotes and voice, and your video if you turn your camera on.

Services that handle data for us

Google Firebase (Authentication and Cloud Messaging)
Sign-in, password resets and Android notifications. Firebase privacy
Cloudflare (Workers, Durable Objects and D1)
Runs our server, the game rooms and our database, hosts the browser version and these pages, and delivers the Android app's updates. Cloudflare privacy
LiveKit Cloud
Carries voice and video between players in a room, live. LiveKit privacy
Free Dictionary API and Wiktionary
Meanings of rarer words. They get only the word, from our server. Wikimedia privacy
Resend
Delivers messages from the contact form to the developer's inbox. Resend privacy

In the browser version, your browser also loads Firebase's sign-in code and some fonts (for example, for emoji) from Google's servers.

Your information is stored and handled outside your province and country, mainly in the United States, where these services run. While it's there, it may be accessible to courts and authorities in those countries. We chose services with strong security, and each one handles our data under its own data-processing terms with us.

No ads, no tracking, no selling

Wordsmith has no ads and no in-game purchases. It contains no analytics, advertising or tracking tools, and doesn't use cookies for advertising or tracking. We never sell your data or share it with advertisers or data brokers. Apart from what other players see, we only share it with the services above, so they can run the game for us, or when the law requires it.

How long we keep it

  • Your account and everything tied to it: until you delete your account. Deleting it removes it straight away; see exactly what's deleted.
  • After you delete: Co-op and Versus results that other players took part in are kept so their history still adds up, with your account removed from them, so nothing in them identifies you (they show “Deleted smith”). Our database host keeps automatic recovery copies for up to 30 days, after which deleted data is gone from those too.
  • Chat: until the room closes.
  • Voice and video: never kept.
  • Reports and blocks: deleted when either player's account is deleted. Reports may be deleted sooner once they've been dealt with.
  • Messages from the contact form: up to a year, then deleted, and sooner once they've been dealt with if you ask. They aren't tied to your account, so deleting your account doesn't remove them: ask in a message and we'll delete them. The scrambled IP address is deleted after a day.
  • Your birth month and year: until you delete your account.
  • Notification tokens: until you sign out, turn notifications off or delete your account, or until Firebase tells us a token no longer works.
  • Server logs: a few days (at most 7).

Your choices

  • Turn off voice chat, text chat, notifications and the daily reminder in Settings. In a room you can also turn your microphone and camera off.
  • Mute a player for the rest of a room by holding down one of their messages. Block or report players from room chat, the lobby or your friends list, and unblock them in Settings › Blocked smiths.
  • Change your username from your profile, and remove friends or nicknames at any time.
  • Delete your account at any time: in the app under Settings › Delete account, or at /delete-account.

Your US privacy rights

Depending on the state you live in (California, Colorado, Connecticut, Texas, Virginia and many others now have privacy laws), you may have the right to:

  • know what personal information we have about you, and get a copy of it;
  • have it corrected or deleted;
  • opt out of its sale, of its use for targeted advertising, and of profiling.

We don't sell your personal information or share it for targeted advertising, and we don't profile you. That goes for every player, including those under 18. Because there's nothing to opt out of, we honour Global Privacy Control and similar signals automatically.

You can see and change most of your information in the game, and delete your account in Settings › Delete account or at /delete-account. For anything else, send a request with the contact form, choosing Privacy and my data. We answer within 45 days.

If we turn a request down, you can appeal by replying to our answer, and we'll respond within 60 days. If you're still unhappy, you can contact your state's attorney general. We'll never treat you differently for using these rights.

Your privacy rights in Canada

Canada's privacy law (PIPEDA) and, in Quebec, the Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector give you the right to:

  • ask what personal information we have about you, how it's used and who it's shared with, and get a copy, including in a structured, commonly used format;
  • have it corrected, or deleted by deleting your account;
  • withdraw your consent, which may mean you can no longer play online;
  • in Quebec, ask us to stop spreading information about you or to remove links to it.

Send a request with the contact form, choosing Privacy and my data. We answer within 30 days. If you're not satisfied with our answer, you can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, or, in Quebec, to the Commission d'accès à l'information.

Children and teens

Wordsmith is for players 13 and over, and 14 and over in Quebec. Online play asks for the month and year you were born, and we don't make accounts for anyone younger or knowingly collect their personal information. Solo play needs no account. If you think a child under 13 has an account anyway, delete it at /delete-account, or contact us and we'll delete it.

Players aged 13 to 17 need a parent's or guardian's permission to play. They get safer defaults: their microphone starts muted in every room, and their camera starts off, as everyone's does. Parents and guardians can contact us about their teen's account at any time.

Security

Every connection to our servers is encrypted (HTTPS and secure WebSockets), and every request that touches your account is checked against your sign-in. Voice and video are encrypted on their way to and from LiveKit. Passwords are handled by Firebase, never by us. No system is perfectly secure, so please use a strong password that you don't use anywhere else.

We keep a record of any security incident involving personal information. If one could seriously harm you, we'll tell you and the privacy regulators as the law requires, and explain what we're doing about it.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we'll update it here and change the date at the top. For important changes, we'll also tell you in the game before they take effect.

Contact

Questions or requests about your privacy or your data: send us a message with the contact form. Leave your email address so we can reply, and your Wordsmith username so we can find your account.

We'll never ask for your password.
