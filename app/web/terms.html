Terms of use

Last updated 19 October 2026

These are the rules for playing Wordsmith, in the Android app and in the browser. By playing, you agree to them. They're short, so please read them, especially the chat rules.

Who can play

  • You must be 13 or older to make an account and play online: friends, rooms, chat, voice and video. When you set up an account we ask the month and year you were born. Anyone can play solo without an account.
  • If you're under 18 (or under the age of majority where you live), you need a parent's or guardian's permission to use Wordsmith, and they agree to these terms for you. Parents and guardians can contact us about their teen's account at any time, or delete it in the app under Settings › Delete account or at /delete-account.
  • Players under 18 join rooms with their microphone muted and turn it on themselves. Cameras start off for everyone.
  • If we learn that an account belongs to someone under 13, we delete it.
  • Wordsmith is offered in every US state and every Canadian province and territory, except that online play isn't offered in Quebec (below). It isn't offered in the European Union or the United Kingdom.
  • Online play isn't offered in Quebec. By making or using an account, you confirm that you don't live in Quebec. We tell where you're playing from by your internet connection's location (see the privacy policy), which can be wrong, for example near the border or on mobile data. If it places you in Quebec and you live somewhere else, you can say so in the game and keep playing. Saying so when you do live in Quebec breaks these terms. Solo play needs no account and is open to everyone.
  • Some US states have laws about teens and apps, for example requiring app stores to share an age range or a parent's consent. When you get Wordsmith from an app store in one of those states, we follow the age and consent information the store gives us.

Using Wordsmith

  • Wordsmith is free to play and has no ads. We may offer optional paid features; the rules for them are in Paid features below. Nothing is for sale yet.
  • Play fairly. Don't try to break, overload or get around how the game works, for example with modified apps, bots or scripts, or by attacking our servers.
  • How we handle your information is explained in the privacy policy.

Your account

  • One person per account. Don't share your account or use anyone else's.
  • Keep your sign-in safe. You're responsible for what's done with your account. If you think someone else has got into it, change your password (Forgot password? on the sign-in screen) and tell us.
  • Your username must follow the rules below. Offensive usernames aren't allowed, even if one gets past the filter.

Chat and voice rules

These apply to everything you share with other players: text chat, voice, video, emotes and your username.

Be kind.

Treat other players the way you'd like to be treated. These are not allowed:

  • Bullying or harassment, including ganging up on someone or following them from room to room.
  • Hate: attacking or mocking anyone for who they are, such as their race, ethnicity, religion, nationality, gender, sexuality or disability.
  • Sexual content of any kind, in words or on camera.
  • Threats, or encouraging anyone to hurt themselves or others.
  • Spam, advertising, or links to scams.
  • Cheating, or helping others cheat.
  • Impersonation: pretending to be another player, a real person or the developer.
  • Sharing personal details such as a real name, address, phone number, school or photo, whether they're someone else's or your own.
  • Offensive usernames.

What we do to keep it friendly

  • Swear words in chat are starred out automatically, and usernames containing them aren't accepted.
  • You can mute anyone in a room by holding down one of their messages, block them (their messages and emotes are hidden everywhere, and you stop being friends), and report them from room chat, the lobby or your friends list.
  • Reports are looked at, and accounts that break these rules can be removed.
  • Voice and video aren't recorded, so if someone breaks the rules there, report them and say what happened.
  • If you think someone is in danger, contact your local emergency services.
